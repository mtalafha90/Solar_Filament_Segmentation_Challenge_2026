\title{Barb-Aware Segmentation of Solar Filaments in Full-Disk H-alpha Observations}

\author{Solar Filament Segmentation Challenge 2026 \\ IEEE Big Data Cup submission}

\begin{abstract}
Solar filaments are cool, dense ribbons of plasma suspended in the corona.
They matter for space weather because their eruption drives coronal mass
ejections, and their fine side-threads, called barbs, encode the chirality of
the magnetic field that supports them. Segmenting filaments is therefore not a
matter of finding dark pixels: the thin structures that carry the science are a
small fraction of the area, and they are exactly what a conventionally trained
network discards. We describe a complete segmentation system built around that
observation. Physically motivated preprocessing removes limb darkening and
atmospheric gradients so that filament contrast becomes uniform across the
disk. A multi-task U-Net, whose bottleneck attention is steered by a learnable
edge map, predicts the filament mask alongside its centreline and boundary.
The training objective combines a centreline Dice term with a recall-weighted
Tversky loss, which together penalise the deletion of a barb far more heavily
than its small pixel count would suggest. A geometric post-processing stage
rejoins fragmented filaments into single objects and rejects sunspots by shape.
We also provide a competitive training-free detector based on multi-scale ridge
filtering and hysteresis thresholding. All components are validated end to end
on synthetic observations that reproduce the relevant properties of GONG data,
and an ablation shows what each contributes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A solar filament is a ribbon of relatively cool, dense plasma held above the
solar surface by the magnetic field. Seen against the bright disk in the
H-alpha line it appears as a dark, elongated, often strongly curved thread.
Filaments matter operationally because their eruption is a principal driver of
coronal mass ejections, which in turn disturb power grids, satellite
navigation, aviation and spacecraft operations \cite{mackay2010}. They also
matter scientifically: the short side-threads that leave a filament's spine at
a steep angle, known as barbs, indicate the chirality of the supporting
magnetic field. Despite this, no operational system currently provides
continuous automated filament detection.

The release of MAGFiLO \cite{magfilo2024} changes what is possible. It provides
10,244 manually annotated filaments across 1,593 full-disk H-alpha observations
from the Global Oscillation Network Group \cite{gong1996}, each with a polygon
mask, a bounding box, a spine and a chirality label, produced over roughly one
thousand person-hours with a double-blind review process.

This paper describes a segmentation system designed around three properties of
the target that ordinary segmentation practice handles badly.

\paragraph{The thin parts carry the information.}
Barbs are a few pixels across. They contribute little to any area-based
overlap measure, so a model optimised for Dice can delete every barb at almost
no cost. This is not a hypothetical failure: it is the default outcome.

\paragraph{The target is rare.}
Filaments cover well under one per cent of the solar disk. Uniform patch
sampling would spend nearly the entire training budget on empty quiet Sun.

\paragraph{Darkness alone is not diagnostic.}
Sunspots are darker than most filaments. Any detector keyed on brightness finds
sunspots first, so they must be separated by shape rather than by contrast.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Photometric preprocessing}

Two effects dominate a raw frame before any filament signal is visible. Limb
darkening makes the disk centre tens of per cent brighter than the edge, so a
filament near the limb is darker in absolute terms than quiet Sun at disk
centre. Ground-based observation adds smooth transmission gradients from thin
cloud, haze and imperfect flat fields, which differ between the six GONG sites
and between successive frames from one site.

We locate the limb by marching outwards along 360 rays and taking the point of
steepest intensity fall-off, refined to sub-pixel accuracy by fitting a
parabola to the gradient peak, then fitting a circle to the crossings with one
round of outlier rejection. The steepest-gradient criterion is preferred to a
fixed half-intensity crossing because limb darkening already dims the disk
substantially before the true edge, which biases any level-crossing inwards.
On synthetic frames this recovers the radius to $0.07\%$ and the centre to
$0.01$ pixels.

We then measure the radial intensity profile as a \emph{median} within radial
bins and divide it out. The median is what makes the estimate robust:
filaments and sunspots are a small minority of pixels in any bin, so they
barely move it. Any remaining large-scale gradient is estimated by normalised
convolution at a length scale far coarser than a filament width and divided out
as well, which flattens the haze while leaving the filaments intact.

The result is that filament contrast becomes uniform across the disk. Measured
in three radial bands on synthetic data, the filament-to-quiet-Sun contrast is
$0.537$, $0.531$ and $0.549$, where the unprocessed image varies by tens of per
cent. A single threshold now carries the same meaning everywhere on the disk.

\subsection{Network}

FilaNet is a U-Net \cite{unet2015} encoder--decoder with residual,
group-normalised blocks. Group normalisation is used because large crops force
small batches, and batch statistics estimated from two or four samples are too
noisy to help. The encoder performs four downsamplings; going deeper enlarges
the receptive field but coarsens the grid past the point where barbs survive.

\paragraph{Edge-guided attention.}
Self-attention at the bottleneck \cite{attention2017} mixes tokens by feature
similarity, and a barb's tokens resemble the quiet Sun around them. We
therefore extract an explicit edge map from the input with a learnable filter
bank initialised to Sobel, Laplacian and ridge kernels, so it begins as a
genuine edge detector \cite{canny1986} and refines from there. That map is
linearly projected and added to the attention Queries and Keys:
%
\begin{align}
Q &= W_q x + W_{q}^{e} E, \\
K &= W_k x + W_{k}^{e} E, \\
V &= W_v x,
\end{align}
%
so tokens on a strong edge attend differently from tokens in a smooth region.
Values are deliberately left untouched: the edge map should determine what
attends to what, not overwrite the semantic content being aggregated. The two
edge projections are zero-initialised, so the block starts as ordinary
self-attention and learns how much edge guidance to admit, which keeps early
training stable. Because the edge map varies with image content, it also
supplies the spatial variation that a learned positional encoding would
otherwise provide. This design follows the direction set by EdgeAttNet
\cite{edgeattnet2025}, at 9.7M parameters against their 22.7M.

\paragraph{Multi-task heads.}
Alongside the mask, the decoder predicts the filament centreline and its
boundary. Both are free to ignore at inference, but they force the shared
decoder to represent the skeleton and outline explicitly. Deep supervision on
the mask at several decoder scales gives the encoder a shorter gradient path.

\subsection{Training objective}

The loss matters more than the architecture. We optimise
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Tv}}
  + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clDice}} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{focal}}
  + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}} ,
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}$ collects the spine, boundary and
deep-supervision terms.

The decisive term is centreline Dice \cite{cldice2021}, computed on
differentiable soft skeletons obtained by iterative morphological thinning. It
scores topology rather than area: deleting a barb breaks a branch of the
skeleton, which clDice punishes heavily even though the pixel count scarcely
moves. On a synthetic filament whose barb accounts for $13.5\%$ of its pixels,
removing that barb raises the clDice loss by a factor of $37$ but the Tversky
loss \cite{tversky2017} by only $7.7$.

Two supporting choices follow the same logic. Tversky with $\beta > \alpha$
weights false negatives above false positives, because a missed barb is a hard
failure against the challenge's fine-structure criterion whereas a few
over-called boundary pixels are cheap. Per-pixel weights derived from a
distance transform concentrate the cross-entropy on outlines and on locally
thin regions, so a two-pixel barb receives the full weighting bonus while the
body of a filament receives almost none. Focal loss \cite{focal2017}
down-weights the easy quiet-Sun pixels that otherwise dominate.

The clDice term is ramped in linearly over the first 500 optimisation steps.
Skeletonising a randomly initialised, nearly uniform prediction is meaningless
and destabilises early training; the topology term is only switched on once the
mask term has produced something with a shape.

\subsection{Sampling and inference}

Because filaments are rare, $70\%$ of training crops are centred on an
annotated filament. The remaining $30\%$ are drawn uniformly from the disk and
are not optional: they are what teaches the model to reject sunspots and plage.

At inference the frame is processed in overlapping full-resolution tiles,
blended with a raised-cosine window that falls to near zero at each tile edge,
so a pixel's prediction is dominated by the tile that saw the most context
around it. Downsampling the frame to fit the network is never acceptable here,
since it destroys precisely the structures being scored. Eight-fold dihedral
test-time augmentation is applied by default.

\subsection{From pixels to filaments}

The challenge asks for each filament as one coherent object, which connected
components do not deliver. A faint waist or a moment of poor seeing splits one
filament into two, costing a detection and adding a false positive. We rejoin
two components when an endpoint of each lies within a set distance \emph{and}
both spines run along the line joining them. Requiring the directions to agree
is what prevents two unrelated filaments passing close by from being welded
together.

Sunspots are then rejected by shape, using second-moment axis ratios, so the
test depends on geometry rather than on a brightness cutoff and transfers
across instruments. Only small components are eligible; a large round region is
more likely to be a genuine filament complex. On synthetic frames with a heavy
sunspot load this reduces the false discovery rate from $0.60$ to $0.14$.

\subsection{A training-free detector}

We also provide a classical detector requiring no training data, useful both as
a reference and as a fallback for observations unlike anything in the training
set. Each pixel is scored by combining a multi-scale Hessian ridge response
\cite{frangi1998}, which responds to elongated structures and largely ignores
round ones, with the local intensity deficit. Hysteresis thresholding then
seeds confident filament cores at a high threshold and grows them through
anything above a much lower one. This is what recovers barbs: a barb never
exceeds the high threshold alone, but it is connected to a core that does,
whereas an isolated noise excursion of the same amplitude is not.

Its single assumption is stated physically rather than hidden in a percentile:
the fraction of the disk covered by filament cores. This matters, because
filament coverage varies by an order of magnitude over the solar cycle, and
seeding $1.2\%$ of the disk when filaments cover $5\%$ caps recall regardless of
the quality of the score map. We evaluated Otsu's method as an automatic
alternative and rejected it: it assumes two classes of comparable size, and on
sparse frames it over-segments badly, estimating $21\%$ coverage for a frame
whose true coverage was $1.2\%$.

\section{Evaluation}

We report the challenge metrics: pixel IoU, precision, recall, average
precision at several IoU thresholds, hit and miss rates, pairwise IoU and
multi-scale IoU (MSIoU) \cite{msiou2021}. We add clDice as a direct read-out of
whether fine structure survived.

MSIoU exists because plain IoU judges thin structures poorly. It compares
Sobel edge maps of the two masks over a ladder of grid resolutions: at fine
grids it behaves like ordinary IoU, while at coarse grids a small offset stops
mattering and a genuinely absent structure still scores nothing. Table
\ref{tab:metrics} shows why this matters.

\subsection{Synthetic validation}

The competition data must be downloaded separately, so all numbers reported
here come from a seeded synthetic generator that reproduces the properties
relevant to algorithm design: a limb-darkened disk, transmission gradients,
photon and detector noise, dark round sunspots, bright plage, a chromospheric
network texture, and dark curved filaments carrying barbs. \textbf{These are
not competition scores}, and the real data is harder in ways synthetic data
cannot capture, above all the irregularity of genuine sunspot groups and the
variability of seeing across the six GONG sites. Their purpose is to
demonstrate that the pipeline is correct end to end and that each component
earns its place.

The expected pattern, and the one observed, is that removing clDice

topological fidelity, which is what the challenge's fine-structure criterion
rewards and what plain IoU is blind to.

\section{Limitations and further work}

Three limitations are worth stating plainly.

First, the classical detector's coverage prior must be set from data. It is
interpretable and easy to measure, but it is not automatic, and a badly chosen
value costs recall directly.

Second, fragment merging is purely geometric. It uses endpoint proximity and
spine alignment, and cannot use the photometric evidence in the gap between two
fragments. Scoring candidate bridges by the image intensity along them would
likely be more reliable.

Third, the chirality labels in MAGFiLO are unused here. Since barbs determine
chirality, and since the barb-preserving losses used here exist precisely to
retain them, adding a chirality classification head is a natural extension that
would also act as a further supervisory signal on the barbs themselves.

\section{Conclusion}

Segmenting solar filaments well is mostly a question of refusing to optimise
the wrong objective. Area-based losses are systematically blind to the thin
structures that carry the scientific content, and no amount of architectural
work fixes that. Combining topology-aware supervision with physically motivated
preprocessing, edge-guided attention and geometric instance reasoning gives a
system that is designed for what the challenge actually scores. A complete
implementation, a training-free baseline, the full metric suite and a synthetic
generator that lets the pipeline be verified without the competition data are
released alongside this report.
